Thunderstorms can be harder on senior dogs because hearing changes, arthritis pain, cognitive changes, medication schedules, and slippery floors can all interact with fear. This guide was checked on 2026-06-17 against AVMA, AAHA, ASPCA, veterinary behavior, and veterinary-hospital resources. It is not veterinary medical advice: sudden new panic, collapse, breathing trouble, injury, destructive escape attempts, or questions about sedatives and supplements belong with a licensed veterinarian who knows the dog.

## Fast decision table

| Situation | Safer choice | Mistake to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Dog hides early in storm | Open the safe room before panic peaks | Waiting until escape behavior starts |
| New or worsening fear | Ask the veterinarian about pain, cognition, and medication options | Assuming it is only stubborn behavior |
| Noise is unavoidable | Buffer sound and reduce visual flashes | Forcing exposure to “toughen up” |
| After storm ends | Offer calm recovery and note triggers | Punishing panting, pacing, or hiding |
